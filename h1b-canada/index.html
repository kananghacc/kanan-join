Why Timing Matters

  • Program capacity limits: Once spots fill, you'll wait until the next intake period (typically 3-6 months)
  • Processing queue: Earlier applications get processed first, reducing your total timeline
  • H1B expiration risk: Starting now protects you if your H1B status changes unexpectedly
  • Policy changes: Immigration programs can change; secure your spot while eligibility is clear

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to the most common questions from H1B holders considering Canada.

You can continue your Canadian application even if your H1B expires. The open work permit application is based on your current or recent H1B status, not your ongoing status. However, we recommend starting the process while your H1B is still valid to strengthen your application. If your H1B expires, you'll need to maintain legal status in the U.S. (through another visa, pending application, or grace period) or return to your home country while the Canadian application processes.

Yes, absolutely. You can continue working in the U.S. on your H1B while your Canadian application is being processed. Many of our clients maintain their U.S. employment until they receive their Canadian work permit or PR. Once you receive your Canadian open work permit, you can work remotely for your U.S. employer from Canada, or transition to a Canadian employer. There's no requirement to quit your U.S. job during the application process.

You can keep your U.S. assets. Your 401k, property, and investments remain yours. However, you'll need to understand tax implications: Canada and the U.S. have a tax treaty, but you may need to file taxes in both countries. Many people keep their 401k in the U.S. and manage it from Canada. Real estate can be rented out or sold. We recommend consulting with a cross-border tax advisor, and we can refer you to trusted professionals who specialize in U.S.-Canada tax matters.

Starting today can save you 2-4 months. Here's why: (1) Document preparation takes 4-8 weeks—starting now means you're ready for the next intake window, (2) Processing queues are first-come-first-served—earlier applications get reviewed sooner, (3) If you wait, you might miss the current intake and wait 3-6 months for the next one, (4) Early submission means faster work permit approval, which starts your Canadian work experience clock earlier for PR points. Our average client who starts immediately gets PR in 14 months; those who delay often take 16-18 months.

No, that's the beauty of the open work permit program. You don't need a job offer to apply. The open work permit allows you to work for any employer in Canada once approved. This gives you flexibility to find the right job after arriving, or even work remotely for your U.S. employer from Canada. However, having a job offer can strengthen your PR application later and may help with certain Provincial Nominee Programs.

You can pursue both paths simultaneously. Applying for Canadian PR doesn't affect your U.S. green card application or H1B lottery chances. Many of our clients keep their U.S. options open while securing their Canadian pathway. This gives you options: if your U.S. green card comes through, you can choose to stay; if not, you have Canada as a backup. Having Canadian PR also doesn't prevent you from later applying for U.S. citizenship if you eventually get your green card. We help you navigate both processes without conflicts.

Our service fees: Vary based on the package (Express Entry, PNP, or comprehensive). We provide a detailed quote during your free consultation with no hidden fees. Payment plans are available. Yes! Your spouse can apply for an open work permit as your dependent, which allows them to work for any employer in Canada. This is one of the major advantages of the program—both partners can work and contribute to your family's income and Canadian experience points. Children can also come with you and attend Canadian schools. When you apply for PR, your entire family is included in the application, and they'll receive PR status when you do.
